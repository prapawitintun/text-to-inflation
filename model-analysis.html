What the calculation means

The solver minimizes membrane strain energy minus pressure × enclosed volume, with the green support vertices fixed. It uses the actual triangles and outward face normals. A deformation or force map is shown only after the equilibrium residual meets the convergence target.

Model assumptions and result interpretation

The 3D reference mesh defines stress-free edge lengths for a geometrically nonlinear, constant-strain triangular membrane with a plane-stress St Venant–Kirchhoff material. For the tension-field option, the material energy is relaxed in compressive principal directions; this approximates slack/wrinkled regions without drawing individual wrinkles. The solver is an educational energy-minimization implementation, not a validated engineering package.

Membrane force is force per unit current width (N/mm), not pressure or a failure score. Green strain is dimensionless and is displayed in percent. Peak values near ideal fixed supports can depend strongly on mesh resolution. Large material strains, self-intersection, loss of stability, or a wrong reference surface can invalidate the physical interpretation even when the numerical residual is small. There is no self-contact or seam-strength check.

A fabricated inflatable needs measured material data, actual restraint conditions, pressure–shape tests, and mesh-convergence checks. A woven fabric needs warp/weft directions and an orthotropic constitutive model; silicone needs a calibrated hyperelastic model. The default result is a response of the stated assumed membrane, not a prediction certified for your physical object.

Method references
  • University of Alberta: constant-strain triangular elements.
  • Abaqus theory: triangular facet elements and rotating local coordinates.
  • Pipkin (1986): relaxed energy for elastic membranes.
  • NASA: nonlinear pressurization and validation of inflatable models.
